Title: Confidential Close Call Transit
Data. 
OMB Control Number: 2138–0044. 
Type of Review: Renewal of
information collection. 
Respondents: WMATA employees. 
Estimated Number of Respondents: 
150. 
Estimated Time per Response: 60
minutes. 
Frequency: Annual. 
Total Annual Burden Hours: 150
hours. 
Abstract: The Confidential Close Call
Program for Transit is a collaborative
program between BTS, WMATA, the
Amalgamated Transit Union (ATU)
Local 689, the International
Brotherhood of Teamsters (IBT) Local
922, and the Office & Professional
Employees International Union (OPEIU)
Local 2. The program provides a
confidential platform to facilitate the
voluntary reporting of close call events
without fear of discipline. The program
was established in 2013 for rail
employees and expanded to include bus
employees in 2016.
Collecting data on the nation’s
transportation system is an important
component of BTS’ mission and
responsibility to the transportation
community as stated in its authorizing
statute (49 U.S.C. 6302). BTS, WMATA,
and the labor organizations that
represent WMATA employees share a
common interest in promoting transit
safety based on timely, accurate, and
relevant data. BTS, a principal statistical
agency within the U.S. Department of
Transportation, serves as the
independent third party for the program
to collect, analyze, and maintain
confidential close call data. Under the
program, a close call is defined as any
situation or circumstance that has the
potential for safety consequences but
did not result in an adverse safety event.
Knowledge about a close call presents
an opportunity to address unsafe work
conditions and practices, prevent
accidents, contribute to decision-
making, and improve overall safety in
the workplace.
BTS collects close call reports
submitted by WMATA employees,
conducts employee interviews, develops
and maintains an analytical database
containing reported data and other
pertinent information, and provides
statistical analysis to WMATA. Data
shared with BTS by WMATA employees
is treated confidentially and protected
by BTS under the Confidential
Information Protection and Statistical
Efficiency Act of 2018 (CIPSEA),
codified as amended at 44 U.S.C. 3561–
3583. Only statistical and non-sensitive
information will be made available
through publications.
Voluntary reporting of close calls to a
confidential system provides a tool to
identify and correct weaknesses within
WMATA and prevents accidents. Close
call reporting fosters a voluntary,
cooperative, non-punitive environment
to communicate safety concerns.
Through the analysis of the data that is
reported, WMATA receives information
about factors that contribute to unsafe
events, which becomes the catalyst to
develop new training programs and
identify root causes of adverse events.
The database also provides valuable
information regarding precursors to
safety risks and contributes to research
and development of intervention
programs aimed at averting accidents
and fatalities.
Employees involved in reporting a
close call incident are asked to fill out
a report and participate in a brief,
confidential interview. Employees
submit the report electronically to BTS.
Reporting employees will be asked to
provide information such as: (1) name
and contact information; (2) time and
location of the event; (3) a short
description of the event; (4) contributing
factors to the close call; and (5) any
other information that might be useful
in determining a root cause for such
events. 
Data Confidentiality Provisions: Data
collected under the Confidential Close
Call Program are protected under
CIPSEA. In accordance with CIPSEA,
these data will be used exclusively for
statistical purposes and will not be
disclosed in identifiable form except
with the informed consent of the
respondent. 
Annual Burden Discussion: It is
estimated that the time to complete a
close call report and participate in a
brief confidential interview will be no
than 60 minutes for a maximum total
burden of 150 hours (150 reports * 60
minutes/60 = 150 hours). Reports are
submitted when there is a qualifying
event, i.e., when a close call occurs.

(4) ways that the burden could be
minimized without reducing the quality
of the collected information. Comments
received after the comment closing date
will be included in the docket and will
be considered to the extent practicable.

SUMMARY : On September 15, 2026, the
OCC, Board, FDIC, and NCUA
(collectively, the agencies) published in
the Federal Register a proposed
interagency guidance and request for
comment on third-party risk
management. The proposed guidance
would reflect the agencies’ supervisory
experience and lessons learned through
examining banking organizations’ third-
party risk management practices and
would discuss the potential benefits that
may accrue when a banking
organization aligns its third-party risk
management practices in relation to the
reasonably assessed risk levels specific
to each of its third-party relationships,
among other things. Public comments
were requested by November 16, 2026.
The agencies have determined that an
extension of the comment period until
December 16, 2026, is appropriate.
